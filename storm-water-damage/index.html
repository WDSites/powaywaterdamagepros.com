Emergency Storm Water Damage in Poway, CA

Wind-driven rain and runoff find the gaps in a house. We clear the water and dry what it reached after a storm in Poway, CA.

Storm water gets into a home in ordinary ways. It comes under a door, through a window seal, down a wall from a failed flashing or across a garage floor when the yard drains toward the house.

If water is entering now, call (760) 769-1159. This page covers the water that comes in during a storm and the drying that follows.

Storm water damage is the wetting of a building by rain, runoff or wind-driven water entering through the roof, walls, doors, windows or grade, and the cleanup and drying that follow.

How storms reach Poway homes

The City of Poway's community profile puts annual rainfall at about 14 inches, and most of it comes in concentrated winter storms. In the January 2024 storm, the National Weather Service reported four-day totals of about 2.47 inches for Poway, according to local broadcast coverage.

That much rain in a short time is enough to overwhelm a clogged gutter or a yard drain. The city's flood prevention page tells owners to inspect and clean rain gutters, rooftops and yard drains before the rains.

Poway's land ranges from 450 to 2,700 feet in elevation according to the same profile, so runoff on a slope moves quickly toward the low point of a lot, which is often a garage door or a patio threshold.

First-hour safety steps during and after a storm

  1. Stay out of water that reaches outlets or a panel. Electricity and storm water are a dangerous mix.
  2. Do not walk through moving water. Even shallow moving water can carry you off your feet and hide debris.
  3. Move people, pets and valuables to higher ground. Lift small items off the floor.
  4. Use sandbags where they help. The city makes sandbags and sand available at three locations when heavy rain is forecast, and they divert water but do not seal it out.
  5. Photograph the damage after it is safe. Include the source if you can see it.

Safety first: storm hazards

Downed lines, loose debris and gas odors are common after storms. Report downed lines to the utility and 911. If water has reached the panel or you smell gas, leave the house before you call anyone else.

How we handle storm water damage

  1. Extract water. Submersible pumps and wet vacuums clear garages, patios and living areas. See emergency water removal.
  2. Check where it came in. Meters and an infrared camera trace the entry point, then follow the water along wall bottoms and up into ceilings.
  3. Remove what cannot be dried. Saturated carpet pad and insulation come out.
  4. Dry the structure. Drying equipment stays until storm-wetted materials read dry by the ANSI/IICRC S500 reference standard, and the process is explained under water mitigation.
  5. Clean and sanitize. Storm water often carries mud and contaminants, so porous materials may be treated as contaminated.

Roof leaks, runoff and floods are different events

Storm water that enters through the roof is a leak. Water that crosses the lot and enters at grade is runoff. Rising water from a creek or storm drain overflow is flooding. Each calls for a different fix and is treated differently by insurers.

A roofer repairs the roof, and a landscaper or drainage contractor corrects grading. We dry and clean the house.

Insurance and storm water

The City of Poway's storm safety page states that standard homeowners insurance does not cover flooding, and that flood insurance through the National Flood Insurance Program has a 30-day waiting period before it takes effect.

Water that enters through a storm-damaged roof is often treated differently from rising water. Read your policy and speak to your insurer, and photograph everything.

Prevention and mistakes to avoid

  • Clear gutters, downspouts and yard drains before the rainy season.
  • Check door thresholds and window seals each fall.
  • Do not wait for a stain to grow before calling.
  • Do not drive through flooded streets.
  • Do not run electrical equipment in a wet room.
