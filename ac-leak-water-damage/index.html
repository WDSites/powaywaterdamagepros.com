AC Leak Water Damage Repair in Poway, CA

A clogged condensate line or a frozen coil can leak for weeks before a stain shows. We dry the structure and repair the damage in Poway, CA.

Air conditioners make water as they cool, and a drain line carries it away. When the line clogs, a pan overflows or a coil ices up, the water has to go somewhere, and it often goes into a ceiling, a wall or a closet floor.

If your AC is leaking now, turn the system off at the thermostat and call (760) 769-1159. The notes below explain what a crew does and where the plumbing and HVAC trades take over.

AC leak water damage is the wetting of ceilings, walls, insulation and floors caused by condensate that escapes from an air conditioner through a clogged drain line, an overflowing pan or a frozen evaporator coil.

How AC leaks happen in Poway homes

Poway's summers are warm. The City of Poway's community profile gives August as the warmest month, with an average high of 82 degrees, and homes in the inland areas around it can run their systems hard through late summer.

That workload matters because algae and dust build up in the condensate line, and a partly blocked line can back water into the pan. An air handler in the attic sits right above a ceiling, so an overflow there shows up as a stain in a bedroom or hallway.

Ductless units on a wall can leak too, usually from a crimped drain tube or a dirty coil. The water runs down the wall and leaves brown trails where it carries dust.

First-hour safety steps for an AC leak

  1. Turn the system off at the thermostat. Then switch off its breaker if water is near the unit.
  2. Stay out from under a bulging ceiling. A swollen ceiling can fall without warning.
  3. Keep water away from outlets and fixtures. Do not touch a light fixture that is wet.
  4. Catch the drip. A bucket and towels slow the spread while you wait.
  5. Photograph the unit, the stain and the pan. The HVAC technician and your insurer will both ask.

How we handle the water damage

  1. Find the extent. Readings around the stain show whether the wet area is a small patch or has spread into the ceiling cavity.
  2. Open what has to open. A bulging or sagging ceiling is cut so it can drain, and wet insulation comes out.
  3. Dry the cavity. A small dehumidifier and a few air movers run in the closet or room, and the crew keeps checking readings. The ANSI/IICRC S500 reference standard guides what counts as dry.
  4. Hand off the HVAC repair. An HVAC technician clears the line or fixes the cause. We do not repair air conditioners.
  5. Repair the surfaces. Drywall, texture and paint are rebuilt once the cavity is dry, described under water damage repair.

Permits and who does what

Building Services at the City of Poway notes that certain permits, such as electrical or mechanical, carry a flat fee. If your air conditioner or its drain line has to be replaced and not just cleared, ask your HVAC contractor how it will be permitted.

The permit list also exempts repairing leaks in pipes, valves or fixtures. A drywall patch is usually finish work, but check with the contractor doing the repair.

Insurance and a leaking air conditioner

A sudden overflow may be treated as accidental discharge. A drip that went on for months is often classed as gradual, and many policies exclude it. The wording on mold, seepage and maintenance varies widely.

A leaking air conditioner is not a flood, and the City of Poway's storm safety page repeats that standard homeowners policies do not cover flooding. Keep service records for the unit, since they help show the damage was sudden.

Prevention and mistakes to avoid

  • Have the condensate line cleared as part of a yearly service.
  • Check the pan under an attic or closet unit each spring.
  • Do not keep running an AC that is dripping.
  • Do not paint over a stain before a moisture reading.
  • Replace a dirty filter on schedule, since a clogged one can ice the coil.
