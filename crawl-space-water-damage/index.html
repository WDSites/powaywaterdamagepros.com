Emergency Crawl Space Water Damage in Poway, CA

Water under a raised floor goes unseen until the floor feels soft or the air smells damp. We extract it and dry the framing above in Poway, CA.

A crawl space is the low area under a raised floor. Many Southern California homes are built on slabs, so crawl spaces are less common here than in other parts of the country, but they do exist under some older and custom homes, and when water gets in, it is easy to miss.

This page covers the water side of the problem: how it gets in, what a crew does, and when mold work comes next. For active water, call (760) 769-1159.

Crawl space water damage is the wetting of soil, insulation, joists, subfloor and ductwork beneath a raised floor, caused by a plumbing leak, drainage that runs toward the foundation, or storm water that enters through vents or a low wall.

How water gets under a Poway home

Blocked drainage sends water toward the structure, and the city's flood prevention page asks owners to keep gutters, roof drains and yard drains clear. On a raised foundation, that water can end up in the crawl space.

Plumbing is the other common source. A drain or supply line under the floor can drip for months, and a hose bib or irrigation line on the outside can feed water to the foundation wall.

The city's community profile puts annual rainfall at about 14 inches, concentrated in the cooler months. That is enough for a lot at the bottom of a slope or a yard that drains toward the house to push water under it during a hard storm.

First-hour safety steps for a wet crawl space

  1. Shut the main water valve if a line may be leaking. A dripping pipe under the floor will keep feeding the problem.
  2. Stay out of the crawl space yourself. It is confined, dark and may hold standing water near wiring.
  3. Keep power away from wet areas. Do not run extension cords or trouble lights into standing water.
  4. Do not walk on a soft floor. Soft spots mean the structure may be weakened.
  5. Photograph from the access hatch. Use a flashlight and do not enter.

Safety first: confined space

A crawl space can hold standing water, loose wiring, sharp objects and poor air. Do not go in to look around. Call and let a crew work with the right protective gear.

How we handle crawl space water

  1. Inspect and map. Meters read the joists and subfloor, and a bright light along the dirt shows standing water and wet insulation.
  2. Extract the water. Submersible pumps and wet vacuums remove standing water. Larger volumes follow the process under emergency water removal.
  3. Remove wet insulation. Insulation that has soaked up water does not recover and holds moisture against the wood.
  4. Dry the framing and soil surface. Fans and dehumidifiers stay on until the joists and soil surface read dry. The ANSI/IICRC S500 reference standard is the guide for that target.
  5. Check for mold. If growth is present, the work moves to a mold remediation scope.

Water is one job and mold is another

Water damage cleanup dries the space and removes what cannot be saved. Mold remediation deals with growth that has already started. The two are separate scopes, and the second is covered under mold remediation.

Under a floor, moisture lingers in dark, still air, so the EPA's 24 to 48 hour drying window is hard to meet without fast extraction.

Insurance and water under the house

A sudden pipe break under the floor is often handled as accidental discharge. Storm water that enters from outside is a different matter. California's Department of Insurance says flood is typically excluded from standard homeowners and commercial policies.

Seepage and long-term moisture are also commonly excluded, so find out how your policy words those terms.

Prevention and mistakes to avoid

  • Keep gutters, downspouts and yard drains clear and pointed away from the house.
  • Check hose bibs and irrigation lines for drips near the foundation.
  • Do not store boxes in a damp crawl space.
  • Do not seal vents or cover the soil without fixing the source first.
  • Do not ignore a musty smell.
