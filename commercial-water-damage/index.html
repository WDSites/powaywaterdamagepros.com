Commercial Water Damage Cleanup and Drying in Poway, CA

A water loss in a business stops work until the space is dry and clean. We cover extraction, drying and cleanup for commercial spaces in Poway, CA.

Water damage in a commercial space brings questions a home does not. Who owns the building, who holds the lease, what the floors and ceilings are made of, and how fast the space has to be usable again.

This page explains what we do on a commercial loss, what we leave to others, and what to settle first. To describe your space, call (760) 769-1159.

Commercial water damage cleanup is the extraction of water, structural drying and cleaning of a non-residential space after a leak, overflow or storm, using the same moisture mapping and drying methods as a home but scoped to the building and its use.

What we do on a commercial water loss

We extract water, map moisture, dry the structure and clean the affected area. The equipment is the same as in a home loss: pumps, extraction units, air movers, dehumidifiers, moisture meters and thermal cameras.

We scope each job from what we find on site. We do not promise an arrival time, and we do not list industries, clients or tenant types, because the work depends on the building and not on who occupies it.

Why commercial losses differ from a house

The City of Poway's community profile describes the Poway Business Park as a 700-acre complex and the city's main commercial area, with more than 500 businesses. Commercial buildings in business parks and shopping areas tend to have larger floor plates, suspended ceilings and shared walls, and water travels differently through them.

Water from an upstairs suite can reach a unit below, and a supply line failure in a shared wall can affect more than one tenant. A moisture map decides where equipment goes, and who pays is often a lease question.

First-hour safety steps for a commercial water loss

  1. Close the water supply. Know where the building's shutoff valves are, and ask the property manager if you do not.
  2. Keep people out of wet areas. Slippery floors, sagging ceilings and wet electrical equipment are all hazards.
  3. Turn off power to wet areas only if it is safe. Do not stand in water to reach a panel.
  4. Protect records and equipment. Move computers, files and stock to dry ground.
  5. Photograph everything and tell the property manager. Notice to the building owner and your insurer is often time sensitive.

Safety first: wet electrical equipment

Water near panels, server racks, point-of-sale equipment or large machines is dangerous. Do not touch anything energized that is wet, and call a qualified electrician if equipment was reached.

How we handle the work

  1. Assess and map. Readings across the floor plate and into adjoining rooms show the true size of the wet area.
  2. Extract. Pumps and extraction units remove water from floors and carpet. See emergency water removal.
  3. Remove what cannot be dried. Wet ceiling tile, insulation and floor covering that cannot be saved are removed.
  4. Dry the structure. Equipment is sized to the space and stays until readings match a dry reference. The ANSI/IICRC S500 reference standard guides the target, and water mitigation describes the process.
  5. Clean. Surfaces are cleaned, and contaminated water is treated as such.

What we do not cover

We do not repair plumbing, rewire equipment, replace roofing or service building systems. We also do not claim certifications, and we do not promise results by a date.

If your space needs a tenant improvement permit for rebuilding, Poway's Building Services page is the contact, and it explains that fees follow the project's valuation.

Insurance, leases and notice

Per the California Department of Insurance, flood is typically excluded under commercial and homeowners policies alike. A burst pipe or an overflow is a different cause and may be covered, depending on the policy.

Commercial leases often say who insures the structure and who insures contents and improvements. Check yours, and notify the building owner and your insurer promptly.

Mistakes to avoid and prevention

  • Waiting for the property manager to decide while water keeps spreading.
  • Running the HVAC system over wet ceiling tile.
  • Moving sensitive equipment onto a wet floor.
  • Discarding damaged items before photographing them.
  • Skipping a regular check of supply lines and drains.
