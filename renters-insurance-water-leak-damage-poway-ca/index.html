Does Renters Insurance Cover Water Leak Damage in Poway, CA?

A rental leak splits the bill between you and the landlord. Here is how it usually divides, and what to do in the first day.

☎ Call (760) 769-1159

By the Poway Water Damage Pros team. Updated 2026.

A leak in a rental home raises two questions at once: who fixes the pipe, and who pays for your wet couch. Renters insurance answers the second one in most cases, but only for certain kinds of leaks and only for your belongings.

This guide explains what a renters policy usually covers after a water leak, what normally stays with the landlord, and what to do in the first day. Policies differ, so treat it as a map and read your own policy for the details.

What a renters policy usually covers

A standard renters policy covers your personal property, such as furniture, clothing and electronics, when it is damaged by a covered event. A burst pipe, a failed water heater or an appliance that overflows are typical covered events when they are sudden and accidental.

Many policies also include loss of use, which can help with hotel costs if the unit cannot be lived in while it is dried and repaired. They also include personal liability, which can matter if water from your unit damages the apartment below. Limits and deductibles vary, so check the numbers on your declarations page.

What it usually does not cover

Renters insurance does not cover the building. The walls, flooring, ceiling and plumbing belong to the landlord, whose own policy handles them. Gradual leaks, such as a slow drip that went unreported for months, are commonly excluded, and flood from outside the home is usually excluded unless you bought separate flood coverage.

Some policies also exclude sewer or drain backup unless an endorsement was added. If your unit took on dirty water, ask your insurer about that wording before assuming it is covered.

The first day: what to do

  1. Close the fixture shutoff or the unit's main valve if you can do so safely, and tell the landlord or property manager right away, in writing if possible.
  2. Photograph and video the water, the source and every damaged item before moving anything.
  3. Keep wet items for the adjuster unless they are unsafe, and make a room-by-room list with approximate values.
  4. Call your renters insurer to open a claim and ask what they require for documentation.

Why speed matters for your claim

Most policies expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage after a loss. Letting carpet soak for days or leaving a wet closet shut can lead to disputes about whether the damage got worse because of delay.

Reporting early also protects your belongings. Cardboard, particleboard furniture and books fail fast when wet. Pull them out, lay papers flat and move furniture to dry ground. If the water is standing, our emergency water removal crew can pump it out and start drying before mold gets a start.
