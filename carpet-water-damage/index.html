Carpet looks like the damage, but the pad underneath holds most of the water. A wet vacuum run across the top leaves the pad and the floor beneath it wet, and that is where mold and odor start.

If your carpet is wet, stop the source and call (760) 769-1159. The notes below explain how a crew decides between drying carpet in place, lifting it and replacing it.

Carpet water damage is the soaking of carpet fibers, backing and pad by water, and the extraction, lifting, drying or removal needed to bring the floor to a safe moisture level.

Why wet carpet is more than a surface problem

Carpet sits on foam or fiber pad, and pad acts like a sponge. Water that soaked through can sit against the subfloor and tack strips for days. Per the EPA's mold guidance, wet carpet must be pulled up and dried before it goes back down.

That guidance also gives 24 to 48 hours as the drying window, and carpet pad holds water long enough to make that tight.

With about 80 percent of Poway's 16,364 housing units single-family, according to the city's community profile, carpet is common in bedrooms and living areas. A leak in a closet or bath often reaches carpet in the next room before anyone sees it.

First-hour safety steps for wet carpet

  1. Stop the source. Close the valve, fix the overflow or stop the water entering.
  2. Keep power away from the wet area. Do not plug in a vacuum or fan on wet carpet that touches outlets, and use a dry outlet only.
  3. Lift furniture off the carpet. Place foil or plastic under legs so stains do not transfer.
  4. Open windows and run the fan only if the air outside is dry. Air helps, but does not dry saturated pad.
  5. Avoid walking on it. Wet fibers and pad break down, and dirty water spreads.

Safety first: dirty water on carpet

If the water came from a toilet, a drain or the outdoors, treat the carpet as contaminated and keep children and pets off it. Do not use a household vacuum on it.

How we handle wet carpet

  1. Extract. A weighted extraction tool pulls water from the carpet and pad. See emergency water removal for deeper water.
  2. Decide what stays. Carpet that took clean water and was caught early can often be lifted and dried. Pad is usually replaced.
  3. Lift and dry. Carpet is peeled back, and air movers and a dehumidifier dry the pad area and subfloor until readings match a dry reference.
  4. Clean. Fibers are cleaned and treated with products suited to the water category.
  5. Reinstall or replace. Carpet is re-stretched and put back, or replaced when it cannot be saved.

Drying decisions are made with the ANSI/IICRC S500 reference standard in hand. Contaminated water changes the answer, as described under water damage cleanup.

Insurance and carpet

A sudden leak or overflow is often covered, and carpet replacement may be paid at an age-based value. Gradual leaks and neglect are commonly excluded.

Flooding from outside is treated differently, since standard homeowners policies leave flood out. Keep photos of the wet carpet before it is removed.

Mistakes to avoid and prevention

  • Do not rent a wet vacuum and call it done.
  • Do not lay a rug or new carpet over a damp floor.
  • Do not leave wet carpet down for days hoping it dries.
  • Check areas near water heaters, washers and window wells for early signs.
  • Do not ignore a musty smell.
