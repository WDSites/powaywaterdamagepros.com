Garden Road is a street the city and the school district both use as a landmark. Garden Road Elementary School is listed by Poway Unified at 14614 Garden Road, and a park directory places Garden Road Neighborhood Park at 14800 Garden Road. The city's own storm-prep landmark on Garden Road

Poway's Winter Storms page names the south side of Garden Road, east of Floral Avenue and across from Garden Road School, as a place to pick up empty bags and loose sand. It tells residents to bring a shovel.

Bags are stocked for residents, and the listing is not a record of flooding at any address. Flood-zone status for this area has not been confirmed, so look up your address on the FEMA flood map.

What the city asks owners to do before rain

The same page asks owners to inspect and clean rain gutters, roof drains and yard drains, to clear brow ditches and private drainage channels of landscape debris and to cover bare soil with plastic held down by sandbags.

Sandbags divert water and do not seal a door, so they help with runoff more than with a flooded room. Rain that gets inside is covered on the storm water page, and bigger events on the flood page.

Indoor leaks are the other half

Not every call after a storm is about the storm. A water heater, an air conditioner drain, a supply line or a washer hose can fail at any time, and a wet ceiling after rain is sometimes a leak from inside.

Tell the dispatcher what you see and when it started. A ceiling stain that appears only in rain points to the roof, and one that grows on a dry day points to plumbing or a condensate line.

Safety steps for the first hour

1

Stay out of moving water

Do not wade into water that is flowing across a yard, garage or driveway.

2

Keep clear of power

Do not touch wet outlets or breakers while standing in water.

3

Close the supply only if safe

If a pipe is the source, close the valve you can reach without entering water.

4

Photograph and call

Record the damage from a safe spot, then call with what you see.

How the crew handles it

Standing water is extracted first. Meters and a thermal camera then trace where it went, and air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure until readings settle. Details are on the water mitigation page and the emergency water removal page.

Insurance and storm records

Photos, dated notes and moisture readings can help describe a loss. Standard homeowners policies often treat flood differently from a sudden plumbing leak, so read your policy and ask your insurer. Records do not guarantee that a claim is approved.
