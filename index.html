Emergency water damage in Poway

What causes water damage in Poway homes

Poway is a city of about 49,000 people in San Diego County, incorporated in 1980. It sits inland in a dry climate, so most of the year's rain falls between November and March. Those months bring roof leaks, window leaks and runoff at doors, and the emergency calls follow the storms.

The first Poway subdivision homes were built and sold around 1959, and tracts kept going up for decades. A house from those years may now have an original water heater, aging supply lines or a roof near the end of its life. Slow leaks inside walls are worth checking for in any older house, and a damp spot near a bathroom or kitchen wall is the usual first clue.

The terrain adds its own risks. Poway Creek runs through the city, and Lake Poway sits inside Poway Community Park behind a dam built in 1972.

Heavy rain in 2017 brought the lake near the top of the dam without a spill, and in 2019 a storm water pipe backed up at the lake's treatment facility. Hard storms can stress local drainage, so check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask the city building department about drainage near your lot.

Homes on slopes toward Twin Peaks and the Blue Sky Ecological Reserve can see runoff move along a slab or behind a retaining wall. Around Old Poway Park and Midland Road, which carry some of the city's oldest history, building ages are mixed, so tell the dispatcher how old your house and its plumbing are. When crews map moisture they check the downhill side of a house, not only the room where the stain showed up.

The emergency line at (760) 769-1159 covers both the dramatic calls, such as a burst pipe at 2 a.m., and the quiet ones, such as a stain spreading across a bedroom ceiling. Either way, the earlier the call, the smaller the scope tends to be.

Insurance claims

Documenting a loss for your claim

Records your insurer will ask for

A burst pipe or a failed appliance is the kind of event homeowners policies tend to pick up. A leak that dripped for months tends to land on the owner, and rising flood water needs its own policy. Your insurer makes the call, so read your policy first. What we supply is the paperwork: photos, moisture readings and a written scope.
