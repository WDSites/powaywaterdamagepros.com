Emergency Sewage Backup Cleanup in Poway, CA

Sewage in a home is contaminated water. We contain it, remove what it soaked and sanitize the area in Poway, CA, with safety first.

☎ Call (760) 769-1159

Sewage backup is not ordinary water damage. The water carries waste and bacteria, so towels and a household mop are not enough, and porous materials it touched usually cannot be saved.

If sewage is coming up through a toilet, tub or floor drain, stop using every fixture and call (760) 769-1159. The pages below explain what happens next and who fixes the blockage.

Sewage backup cleanup is the safe containment, extraction, removal of contaminated materials and sanitizing of an area that waste water has entered through a toilet, drain or sewer line.

Why sewage backs up and who is responsible

The City of Poway's Wastewater division runs the collection system, which its sewer page puts at 186 miles of pipeline, and the wastewater is treated by the City of San Diego or the City of Escondido. A blockage in that system can push waste back toward homes at the low end of the line.

Many backups start on the property instead. Roots, grease and so-called flushable wipes can block the lateral pipe, and the city asks residents not to flush wipes or pour cooking grease down drains.

Responsibility splits at the cleanout. The city's sewer lateral page says it takes responsibility from the property-line cleanout to the sewer main, and the portion between your house and the cleanout is the owner's. A plumber can tell you where the blockage sits.

First-hour safety steps for a sewage backup

  1. Stop using water. Do not flush, run sinks, showers or the washer, since each adds to the backup.
  2. Keep people and pets out of the area. Children and anyone with a weak immune system should stay away.
  3. Do not touch the water with bare hands. Contaminated water carries bacteria.
  4. Avoid electrical hazards. Turn off power to the affected area only if the panel is reachable on dry ground.
  5. Do not run the HVAC system. It can spread contamination and odor through the house.
  6. Call a plumber and then us. The line has to be cleared before cleanup can finish.

Safety first: contaminated water

The EPA notes that standing water and wet materials are a breeding ground for viruses, bacteria and mold. Do not try to sanitize with a mop and bleach alone, and do not wet vacuum sewage with a household machine.

How we clean up after a sewage backup

  1. Contain the area. Plastic sheeting and tape close off the affected rooms so contamination does not travel.
  2. Extract the water. Pumps and extraction units, run with protective gear, remove the liquid.
  3. Remove contaminated porous materials. Carpet, pad, lower drywall and insulation that touched sewage are bagged and removed.
  4. Clean and disinfect. Hard surfaces and framing are cleaned and treated with products made for the job.
  5. Dry the structure. Wall and floor materials that can be saved are dried in place, and readings are logged each visit until they match unaffected areas, as the ANSI/IICRC S500 reference standard describes for category 3 losses.
  6. Deodorize. Odor control follows once the source and wet materials are gone.

Sewage is treated as the most contaminated category of water, which is why this work differs from water damage cleanup after a clean supply line leak. If floodwater and sewage mixed, the wider loss is handled under flood damage restoration.

Insurance and sewer backups

Coverage for sewer and drain backup is often an endorsement, not part of a standard homeowners policy. Check your declarations page, because some policies exclude it unless it was added.

A backup is not a flood in the insurance sense, but wording varies by carrier. Document the damage, keep receipts and ask your insurer before you discard anything.

Mistakes to avoid and prevention

  • Do not run water to see whether the drain clears.
  • Do not walk through the area in street shoes and then through the house.
  • Do not flush wipes, even those labeled as flushable.
  • Do not pour grease down drains.
  • Know where your cleanout is, and keep it accessible.
