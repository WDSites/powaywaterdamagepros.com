Emergency water removal is the extraction step. Pumps and wet vacuums pull the loose water out first, because every hour it stands, it soaks deeper into drywall, pad and subfloor.

Emergency water removal, also called water extraction, is the use of submersible pumps, truck-mounted or portable extraction units and wet vacuums to remove standing water from a structure before drying begins.

When standing water is an emergency

Standing water is an emergency from the first inch. An inch of water across a 1,000 square foot floor is hundreds of gallons, and it wicks upward into walls and cabinets within hours.

It is more urgent when the water is near electrical outlets, when it came from a drain or toilet, or when it is deepening. Do not wade through it to reach a panel or appliance. If the panel is in the water, leave it, and call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas concern before you call us.

Safety first: water and electricity

Stay out of standing water if anything electrical is touching it, including extension cords and floor outlets. Cut power at the panel only if you can reach it while standing on dry ground. If you smell gas or see sparking, leave the building and call 911 or your utility before calling the restoration line.

How extraction works

Crews match the equipment to the depth and the surface. Submersible pumps move deep water quickly. Portable extraction units pull water from carpet and pad. Truck-mounted units handle large areas where hose reach and volume matter.

Hard floors are squeegeed and vacuumed. Carpet is extracted in passes, then the pad is checked, since pad holds the most water and is usually removed. When the loose water is gone, moisture mapping shows what is still wet, and drying equipment goes in. That hand-off is covered under structural drying.

Burst pipe water damage

A burst pipe is the classic extraction call. A failed supply line under normal household pressure can release a large volume of water in an hour, and the water usually runs behind walls and down through ceilings before it shows. The first move is the main shutoff valve, which is typically at the front of the house, at the meter or at the side of the garage.

After the valve is closed, open a faucet at the lowest point to drain the line, and photograph everything before you move it. When we arrive, crews trace the water path with a moisture meter, extract what is standing, open wet ceilings that are bulging so they can drain, and set drying equipment. A plumber repairs the line. The restoration crew does not repair plumbing, and we say so up front.

Insurance, in general terms

A sudden pipe or appliance failure is often covered by a homeowners policy. Water that enters from outside as rising flood water is usually excluded and needs flood coverage. Check your policy, take photos before cleanup, and keep receipts. When the water is out and the structure is drying, water damage cleanup handles removal and sanitizing.
