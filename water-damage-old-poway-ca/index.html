Old Poway is the older core of the city. The City of Poway's own Old Poway Park page calls Midland Road the center of the Old Poway area and says it runs from Twin Peaks Road in the north to Hilleary Place in the south. The city has not published a formal boundary, so treat that stretch of Midland Road as a landmark and not a line on a map.

If water is spreading in a home or building near there, call (760) 769-1159. The dispatcher confirms your address, asks what the source is and explains what to do while a crew is arranged.

What stands in Old Poway, and why building age matters

The city's park page says Old Poway Park covers nearly five acres, that the city bought the land from a private estate in December 1986 and that the park opened in 1993. Buildings there such as Templar's Hall and the Nelson House were moved and restored from other parts of Poway. Midland Elementary School, in the Poway Unified directory, sits at 13910 Midland Road.

Nearby buildings tend to be a mix of ages, with an original structure, a later addition and plumbing that was updated in one wall and not in the next. We do not assume that age means more leaks. We do ask when the building went up and what has been renovated, because the material decides how a crew dries it.

Historic buildings: ask the city before you rebuild

Chapter 17.45 of the Poway Municipal Code sets a process for historic and cultural resources. It requires a certificate of historical significance before alterations to a designated landmark and controls demolition of landmarks and of resources ranked A or B.

If your Old Poway property may be designated, tell the dispatcher so the crew can plan around original materials. Then ask the city's Development Services staff what approvals rebuilding would need. A landmark designation is a question for the city, not for us.

Storm prep near Midland Road

Poway's Winter Storms page also names the Farmer's Market parking lot on the west side of Midland Road, south of Old Poway Park, as a sand and bag pickup point. A pickup point is not evidence that any address floods.

The same page asks owners to clean gutters, roof drains and yard drains, and says private owners maintain the drainage courses and storm drains on their own property. We have not confirmed which FEMA flood zone, if any, covers a given Old Poway address, so look yours up on the FEMA flood map.

Where water damage starts in a building like this

Any older building can have a failed supply line, a leaking water heater, a worn appliance hose or a clogged air conditioner drain. A roof that has been patched over the years can also let water in during a storm.

A split line is covered on the burst pipe page, and a leaking tank on the water heater page. If the water is already standing, emergency water removal comes first.

Safety steps while you wait

1

Stop the source

Close the main valve or the fixture shutoff only if you can reach it safely.

2

Stay out of the water near power

Do not touch outlets, cords or breakers while standing in water. Wait for a professional.

3

Photograph first

Take pictures of the damage and the source before anything is moved.

4

Keep people clear

Keep children and pets away from standing water, then call.

How extraction and drying work

The crew pumps or extracts standing water first, then maps where the moisture went with meters and a thermal camera. Air movers and dehumidifiers are placed to dry floors and walls, and readings are repeated until the structure matches a dry reference. The ANSI/IICRC S500 reference standard is the guide for what counts as dry.

Old flooring and plaster may not be treated the same way as drywall and new flooring. The full method is on the water mitigation page, and rebuilding is covered under water damage repair.

Records that help with an insurance claim

Photos, moisture readings and a written scope of the work can help describe what happened. Whether a claim is covered depends on the cause and on your policy, so read it and ask your insurer. We do not decide coverage, and records do not guarantee payment.
