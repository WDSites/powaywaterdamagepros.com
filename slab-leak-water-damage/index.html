Emergency Slab Leak Water Damage in Poway, CA

A leak in a line under the concrete shows up as warm spots, damp baseboards or a high water bill. We dry the home it reached in Poway, CA.

A slab leak is a leak in a pressurized line that runs in or under a concrete floor. You do not see it burst. You see its effects, such as a damp patch on carpet, baseboards that swell, a floor that feels warm or a meter that keeps turning with everything off.

If you suspect one, close the main valve and call (760) 769-1159. A crew handles the water damage while a plumber finds the line.

Slab leak water damage happens when a supply or hot water line under or within a concrete slab leaks and the water rises to the surface or travels sideways and wets flooring, walls and subfloor.

How common slab leaks are in Poway, and why

Slab foundations are widely used across Southern California, and the City of Poway's community profile shows about 80 percent of its 16,364 housing units are single-family homes. Where a house sits on a slab, its water lines may run through or beneath the concrete.

That makes a slab leak a real local issue, though not every home has the layout. Houses on raised foundations route lines under the floor instead, and that case is covered under crawl space water damage.

Leaks begin when a copper line corrodes, rubs against the slab or fails at a joint. Hot water lines wear faster, which is why a warm floor patch is a classic clue. Pinhole leaks can run for a long time before a bill or a stain gives them away.

First-hour safety steps for a suspected slab leak

  1. Close the main water valve. Do this even if you are not sure. It stops the supply to every line.
  2. Turn off the water heater supply and its energy source. A hot water line leak can be fed by the tank.
  3. Keep power away from wet floors. Do not use extension cords on a damp slab, and cut power to the wet room only if the panel is dry to reach.
  4. Lift rugs and furniture legs off the wet area. Wood furniture stains quickly.
  5. Note the meter reading. A plumber uses it to confirm the leak, and an insurer may ask.

How we handle the water damage

  1. Map the moisture. Meters on the slab surface and at the base of each wall show how far the moisture has crept from the leak.
  2. Extract any surface water. Wet vacuums and extractors clear carpet, pad and hard floors. See emergency water removal.
  3. Remove what cannot dry. Carpet pad, swollen baseboard and wet insulation at the wall base come out.
  4. Dry the slab and the walls. Concrete holds water, so air movers and desiccant or LGR dehumidifiers run longer than on a framed floor.
  5. Confirm with readings. Equipment stays until the readings reach a dry target under the ANSI/IICRC S500 reference standard.

Finding and fixing the line is the plumber's job

Leak detection uses sound, pressure and thermal methods, and the plumber decides whether to reroute the line through the attic or walls or to break the slab and repair it. We do not do either.

Under the city's permit guidance, minor leak repairs are exempt but a new concealed pipe is not, and rerouting a line through a wall or attic counts as new work. Ask your plumber what applies.

Insurance and slab leaks

Coverage varies a great deal. Many policies cover the water damage from a sudden leak but treat the cost of finding and tearing into the slab differently, and a leak that went on for months may be treated as gradual damage.

A slab leak comes from plumbing, so it is not a flood claim. Ask your insurer how your policy treats leak detection and access to the line, since those costs are often handled differently.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Waiting to see whether the damp spot dries on its own.
  • Covering a wet floor with new flooring before it is read with a meter.
  • Ignoring a warm patch on a hard floor.
  • Replacing carpet before the slab has dried.
  • Assuming a high water bill is just seasonal use.
