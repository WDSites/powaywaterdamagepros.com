Older homes, older plumbing: where Escondido water damage starts

The Old Escondido Historic District near downtown has single-family homes from the late 1800s and early 1900s, mostly Victorian and Craftsman in style. Houses that age often have original wood framing, plaster walls and later plumbing add-ons, so a slow leak can sit inside plaster for months before it stains.

Plaster dries slower than drywall, and old wood floors can cup or crown. If your Escondido home predates the 1960s, tell the dispatcher, because the drying plan and the equipment change.

When water is spreading in Escondido

Water becomes an emergency when it is still running, when it comes through a ceiling, or when it reaches electrical items. Shut the main valve first if a pipe is the source, keep out of standing water near power, and call us before you start moving furniture.

Inland Escondido summers are hotter than the coast, so air conditioner condensate lines clog and back up into closets and ceilings. That is a frequent cause of mold in this part of the county, and the fix starts with finding the water source.

1

Stop the source

Close the main water valve or the fixture shutoff. Tell the dispatcher if you cannot find it.

2

Protect the power

Switch off power to wet rooms only if you can reach the panel while standing on dry ground.

3

Lift what you can

Raise small valuables, papers and electronics off wet floors. Photograph everything first.

4

Keep people clear

Keep children and pets out of dirty water, and call the 24/7 line.

Escondido Creek, storms and flood moments

Escondido Creek runs through the middle of the city, from Lake Wohlford in the northeast through downtown and out toward Harmony Grove. It was turned into a concrete flood-control channel in the 1960s, and a bike path follows much of it. More than 80 percent of the city's rain falls between November and March, and records for the area include a single-day total of more than six inches in 1927.

Rare as that is, it shows that a storm can drop a season of rain in a day. Check the FEMA flood map for your address, ask the City of Escondido building department about drainage near your lot, and ask your insurer about flood coverage, which standard policies usually exclude.

What we run in Escondido

Most calls here begin with flood damage restoration when storm water or a sewer backup is involved, or water mitigation when a leak has soaked a wall or floor. When the water is standing, emergency water removal pumps it first, and water damage cleanup follows for anything contaminated.

The city's water is supplied mainly by the City of Escondido's utilities department, and parts of the wider valley by Rincon del Diablo Municipal Water District. Ask your bill which one serves you, since that tells you where the shutoff and meter sit.
