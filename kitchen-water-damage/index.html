Kitchen Water Damage Repair and Drying in Poway, CA

Kitchens have more water lines in a small space than any other room. We dry the cabinets, floors and walls after a leak in Poway, CA.

The kitchen holds a sink, a dishwasher, a refrigerator water line and often a garbage disposal, all inside a few feet of cabinets. A leak at any one of them can run unseen behind a toe kick for weeks.

If water is on the floor now, close the nearest valve and call (760) 769-1159. The sections below explain what a crew does and how the room is brought back.

Kitchen water damage is the wetting of cabinets, flooring, subfloor and walls in a kitchen after a failed supply line, drain, dishwasher, disposal or refrigerator line, and the drying and repair that follow.

How kitchen leaks start and where the water goes

Common sources are a split sink supply line, a loose drain trap, a dishwasher hose or door seal, a disposal that has failed and an ice maker line behind the refrigerator. Most are small at first and grow slowly.

The City of Poway reports roughly 80 percent of its housing is single-family, and many of those kitchens have tile or laminate over a slab or a raised subfloor, which hides how far water has gone.

Water follows the cabinet base. It slides under toe kicks, wicks into particle board and moves along the subfloor to the dining area. The cabinet interior can look dry while the base is saturated.

First-hour safety steps for a kitchen leak

  1. Close the closest shutoff. Sinks, dishwashers and refrigerator lines have their own valves. If you cannot find one, use the main valve.
  2. Turn off the dishwasher and unplug small appliances. Only with a dry outlet, and not while standing in water.
  3. Mind the electrical hazard. Kitchen outlets sit close to the counter and the floor. Cut power at the panel if water reaches them and the panel is dry to reach.
  4. Empty the cabinet. Remove items from the wet base so it can be inspected.
  5. Photograph the leak, cabinet interior and floor. Insurers ask for them.

How we dry and repair a kitchen

  1. Extract. Wet vacuums and extractors clear the floor and the edges of the cabinets.
  2. Pull the toe kicks and open the base. This exposes the subfloor and lets air in.
  3. Map the moisture. Readings along the cabinet bases and into the next room show where the wet area ends.
  4. Remove what cannot be saved. Swollen particle board, wet insulation and damaged flooring are removed.
  5. Dry the cabinets and floor. Equipment stays until readings are normal under the ANSI/IICRC S500 reference standard, and the full method is under water mitigation.
  6. Repair. Cabinet bases, flooring, drywall and paint are rebuilt after readings match, as described under water damage repair.

Permits and finish work

The city's permit list leaves out finish work such as painting, cabinets and countertops. Plumbing is different, and concealed pipe replaced with new material is new work that needs a permit.

Your plumber can tell you which category the repair falls into, and the city's Building Division can confirm.

Insurance and kitchen leaks

A sudden failure such as a split supply line or a burst dishwasher hose is often covered as accidental discharge. A leak that went on for weeks under a sink is often treated as gradual and may be excluded.

Cabinet and counter replacement may also be handled separately. A kitchen leak is not a flood. Read your policy for how it treats a sudden plumbing discharge.

Mistakes to avoid and prevention

  • Do not replace flooring before the subfloor has been read with a meter.
  • Do not leave items stored under the sink, since they hide leaks.
  • Check supply lines and traps every few months.
  • Do not run a dishwasher that is leaking.
  • Do not cover a wet toe kick with new trim.
