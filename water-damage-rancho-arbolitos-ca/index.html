Rancho Arbolitos is a name the City of Poway itself uses. It appears on two city facilities, Arbolitos Mini Park at 12495 Ted Williams Parkway and the Arbolitos Sports Fields at 14401 Pomerado Road. Start with the sewer: who owns which pipe

Poway's Wastewater division runs the city sewer system, which the city's sewer page puts at 186 miles of pipeline. A backup can start in that system or on your own property, and the difference matters for who clears it.

The city's sewer lateral page says its responsibility runs from the property-line cleanout to the sewer main, and the pipe between your house and the cleanout belongs to the owner. A plumber can tell you where the blockage sits. If wastewater has come up through a floor drain, toilet or tub, treat it as contaminated and see the sewage backup page.

What the city says about drainage on your lot

The city's Winter Storms page says private owners maintain drainage courses, storm drains and creeks on their own property or easements, and city staff maintain them on public property. It also asks owners to clear gutters, roof drains and yard drains before storms.

How cleanup differs when the water is not clean

Contaminated water is removed with extraction equipment and porous materials such as carpet pad are usually taken out, because they cannot be reliably cleaned. Surfaces are cleaned and sanitized, then dried with air movers and dehumidifiers, using the ANSI/IICRC S500 reference standard as a guide.

Ordinary household fans and vacuums are not enough for sewage or contaminated floodwater. The crew's steps for clean leaks are on the water damage cleanup page and the larger events on the flood page.

Insurance paperwork after a sewer or plumbing event

Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope can help describe the damage and the work. Coverage depends on the cause and on your policy terms, and sewer backups are often handled under a separate endorsement, so ask your insurer before you assume either way. Records do not guarantee reimbursement.
