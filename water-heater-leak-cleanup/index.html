Cleanup is the part that follows. This page explains what a leaking heater does to a house, what a crew dries, and what is left to your plumber.

Water heater leak cleanup is the removal and drying of water that escaped from a tank, a supply connection or a relief valve line, along with the flooring, walls and stored items it reached.

Why water heaters leak and where the water goes

Tank heaters wear out from the inside. Sediment and corrosion eventually open a seam or loosen a fitting, and the first sign is often a damp floor under the tank. Supply connectors, drain valves and temperature relief lines are the other usual leak points.

Where the heater sits decides the damage. A garage unit wets a slab and the bottom of the wall. A closet unit soaks drywall and the room beside it. An attic unit sends water through a ceiling.

California law requires residential water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake movement, according to Health and Safety Code section 19211. A strapped heater is less likely to shift and tear its own connections, and sellers must certify the bracing to a buyer.

First-hour safety steps for a leaking water heater

  1. Shut the cold water supply. The valve is usually on the pipe at the top of the tank. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
  2. Turn off the energy source. Switch off the breaker for an electric heater. For a gas heater, turn the gas control to off if you can reach it safely.
  3. Leave if you smell gas. Go outside and call 911 or the gas utility before anything else.
  4. Stay out of standing water near the unit. Water around an electric heater or its wiring is an electrical hazard.
  5. Photograph the tank, the label and the water. The age on the label helps your claim.

Safety first: hot water and gas

Water from a failed heater can be hot, and a gas heater adds a fuel risk. Do not relight a pilot in a wet room, and do not use the unit again until a licensed plumber has checked it.

How we clean up after a water heater leak

  1. Extract the water. Wet vacuums and portable extractors remove water from the slab or floor around the tank. Larger spills are pumped. See emergency water removal.
  2. Check the framing and drywall. A meter finds wet wall bottoms and insulation, and infrared imaging shows where the water ran along the floor.
  3. Remove what cannot be dried. Soaked carpet pad, swollen baseboard and wet insulation come out.
  4. Dry the closet or garage wall. The crew sets a dehumidifier and a small number of fans, then rechecks readings until the wall base is dry, using the ANSI/IICRC S500 reference standard as the yardstick.
  5. Clean and deodorize. Surfaces are cleaned. Stored items are sorted into those that can be dried and those that cannot. The wider process is in water damage cleanup.

Replacement and permits

A restoration crew does not replace the heater. A licensed plumber does that, and the City of Poway's permit list does not name water heater replacement as an exempt item, so the city's Building Division is the place to confirm requirements.

Poway's Building Services page ties permit fees to project valuation and lists an inspection request line, so ask your plumber who pulls the permit.

Insurance and a failed water heater

A sudden failure is often treated as accidental discharge, and the cleanup of its water damage is commonly part of a claim. The tank itself is usually treated as wear and tear and is not paid for.

A heater that has been weeping for months is judged differently, and many policies exclude gradual damage. It is also not a flood. Photograph the label and the damage and read your policy.

Prevention and mistakes to avoid

  • Check the tank base, the supply connections and the relief line pipe once or twice a year.
  • Keep boxes and paper off the garage slab around the heater so a leak is easy to see.
  • Know where the cold supply valve is before you need it.
  • Do not drain or refill a damaged tank without a plumber.
  • Do not ignore a puddle that dries and returns.
