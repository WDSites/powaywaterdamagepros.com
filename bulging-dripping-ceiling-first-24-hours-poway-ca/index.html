Bulging or Dripping Ceiling in Poway, CA: The First 24 Hours

A ceiling that bulges or drips needs fast, safe action. Here is the order of steps.

☎ Call (760) 769-1159

By the Poway Water Damage Pros team. Updated 2026.

A dripping or bulging ceiling is an emergency. The weight of trapped water can bring drywall down, and water moving through ceiling cavities can reach light fixtures and wiring. Acting in the first hours limits damage to the ceiling, the insulation above and the floor below.

This guide covers the safe steps in order, and how to decide who to call first.

Safety first

If the water is reaching a light fixture, ceiling fan or outlet, do not use that switch. Turn off power to that circuit at the panel only if you can reach it while standing on dry ground. If you smell burning or see sparks, leave and call 911 or your utility first.

Step by step: what to do now

  1. Clear the floor. Move furniture and valuables away from under the leak. Cover large items with plastic.
  2. Place a bucket and tarp. Catch drips and protect the floor from splashing.
  3. Find the source. Is it above a bathroom, kitchen or laundry, or is it raining outside?
  4. Stop the water if it is plumbing. Close the fixture valve or the main valve.
  5. Relieve a bulge carefully. If a ceiling is bulging with water, stand to the side, not under it, and poke a small hole at the lowest point of the bulge with a screwdriver, with a bucket below, so the water drains in a controlled way.
  6. Photograph everything and call the 24/7 line.

Relieving a bulge is a judgment call. If the ceiling looks ready to collapse, leave the area and call for help rather than reaching under it.

Restoration crews do not repair roofs or plumbing, and we say so on the first call. What we do is remove the water, open wet ceilings that need to drain, dry the cavity and protect the room. The source still needs its own trade.

After drying, rebuilding is handled under water damage repair, and for the drying itself see water mitigation.

What happens in the first 24 hours

Within the first day the crew drains trapped water, removes saturated insulation, opens soft drywall and begins drying. Moisture readings establish a baseline and the equipment runs from that point.

If you wait, wet insulation stays wet and the ceiling drywall softens. A small leak that reaches that stage becomes a larger repair, and the room below can pick up mold risk.

After the water is out: what not to do

  • Do not paint over the stain. Paint traps moisture and hides a wet cavity.
  • Do not turn on a ceiling fan or light fixture that was wet until it has been checked.
  • Do not walk into the attic above the leak without a safe path on the joists.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum wet insulation. Wet insulation is heavy and sheds fibers when disturbed.
  • Do not wait for the stain to stop growing before calling. A stain that stops can still hide wet material.

Once the water is controlled, drying starts and the damaged section is opened if it will not dry in place. Keep photos of each stage for your records.
