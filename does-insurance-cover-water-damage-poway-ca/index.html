Does Insurance Cover Water Damage? A Poway, CA Homeowner Guide

Coverage depends on cause. Here is how the common patterns work, and how to build a claim file.

☎ Call (760) 769-1159

By the Poway Water Damage Pros team. Updated 2026.

Most homeowners policies cover some water damage and exclude other kinds, and the line between them is about cause, not about how wet the house got. Knowing which side your loss falls on before you call your insurer helps you describe it accurately.

We are not your insurer or an attorney, and your policy governs. This guide explains the common patterns so you know what to ask.

Sudden and accidental versus gradual

Policies commonly cover sudden and accidental discharge, such as a supply line that bursts, a water heater that fails or a washing machine hose that lets go. The damage is covered because the event was unexpected.

Gradual leaks, such as a slow drip under a sink that went unnoticed for months, are often excluded as maintenance issues. If a leak was ongoing, an insurer may argue the damage was preventable, so how you describe the timeline matters.

The first steps in a claim

  1. Protect the house. Stop the source and begin drying. Policies expect reasonable steps to prevent more damage.
  2. Document before cleanup. Take photos and video of the source, the water and every damaged item.
  3. Call your insurer. Ask about the claim process, your deductible and what they need.
  4. Keep records. Save receipts, moisture readings, scope notes and a list of items thrown away.
  5. Ask for the scope in writing. Compare it against the adjuster's estimate.

What documentation helps

Moisture readings show how far the water traveled, and daily drying logs show that the structure was handled properly. A written scope shows what was removed and why. Those documents make it easier to see whether an estimate matches the real work.

We provide photos, moisture readings and a written scope. We do not bill insurers directly or promise how a claim will turn out, because coverage decisions belong to your insurer.

Flood is a separate question

Standard homeowners policies generally exclude flood, meaning surface water rising from outside, and a separate flood policy is needed. The National Flood Insurance Program is run through FEMA, and private flood policies also exist. Flood coverage often has a waiting period before it takes effect, so buying after a storm is announced is usually too late.

Ask your insurer whether your policy has a sewer or drain backup endorsement. That coverage is separate from flood and is often missing.

Common claim mistakes

  • Waiting to start drying until the adjuster arrives, which lets the loss grow.
  • Throwing away damaged items before photographing them.
  • Describing a long-running leak as sudden, which can create problems later.
  • Assuming flood and sewer backup are covered without checking for endorsements.
  • Signing a work authorization before you understand the scope.
  • Not asking your insurer for the claim number and adjuster contact in writing.

Being accurate matters more than being persuasive. Describe what you saw, when you first saw it and what you did next. Your insurer decides coverage, and a clear timeline makes the conversation shorter.
